# Research prompt — music you cannot see stateside, Tokyo & Kansai, 19–26 Sept 2026

Paste the section below into a fresh agent/session. It is self-contained.
Written 2026-09-05 as the second music brief for this trip. The first brief
(`music/jazz/instructions/japan-sept2026-jazz-research-prompt.md`) covered jazz and was
executed on 2026-08-30/31; its output is the "Silver Week Jazz Book". This brief is
deliberately NOT jazz and deliberately NOT ranked by prestige or by the listeners' standing
taste. The single question it answers is: **what live music in the window could two people
based in New York not see at home?**

---

## THE TASK

Find every live music event in Tokyo (19–22 Sept 2026) and Kansai (23–25 Sept 2026) that a
New York resident could not reasonably experience without flying to Japan. Rank them by how
irreproducible they are, report availability and price with sources, and say plainly how
each one gets booked by a foreigner.

Report facts as of the day you run this. Do not buy, reserve, or log in anywhere. Do not
enter personal data or submit any form.

**Jazz is out of scope.** It has already been researched to death for this window. Do not
re-check jazz clubs or jazz kissa. The only jazz-adjacent thing you may mention is a
crossover that is itself Japan-only (min'yō-jazz, Okinawan-jazz, a Japanese-instrument
improv set at an experimental room), and then in one line.

## THE LISTENERS

understand (noh, gagaku, bunraku are fine; nobody needs an English earphone guide, though
say when one exists). Vegan, which only matters if a venue is a dinner package: check the
menu before recommending the food, and prefer music-only tickets.

They live in Brooklyn and go out a lot. **The New York baseline they can already get:**
Japan Society and Asia Society programmes; Blue Note and Village Vanguard; Kodo and Drum

galleries. **Anything of that shape does not clear the bar**, but it can be mentioned in 
an appendix if outstanding.

Their standing taste (modal/post-bop jazz, Latin, Japanese fusion, jazz-funk) is
**background only**. Do not rank by it. Do not use it to exclude anything. Use it at most
as a final tiebreaker between two otherwise equal picks.

Based Shibuya 19–22 Sept, downtown Kyoto (Kawaramachi/Karasuma) 23–25 Sept. Departing
KIX early Sat 26.

## WHAT "CANNOT SEE STATESIDE" MEANS

Apply this test to every candidate: **could a Brooklyn resident see essentially this
within a normal year without flying?** If yes, it does not belong on the shortlist,
whoever is playing.

Three tiers, best first:

- **Tier 1 — the form or the setting exists only in Japan.** Gagaku or bugaku performed
  at a shrine or temple as part of a rite, not a concert; noh with its hayashi and chant
  in a purpose-built nōgakudō; kabuki's nagauta and geza music at Kabuki-za; bunraku's
  gidayū; a min'yō or Tsugaru-shamisen izakaya with resident players; an Okinawan
  sanshin izakaya; a meikyoku kissa (classical listening café); a Buddhist morning
  service with chant open to the public; a shrine festival with matsuri-bayashi or
  kagura; a Koenji or Shimokitazawa live-house bill; Dommune with a live audience; an
  enka or kayōkyoku show; underground idol at a theatre; chindon-ya.
- **Tier 2 — the artist essentially never leaves Japan.** Domestic-only legends,
  Kansai underground lifers, enka singers, city-pop-era veterans, Japanoise and
  onkyō players who tour Europe at most, regional folk troupes.
- **Tier 3 — Japan-flavoured but exportable.** Taiko troupes that tour, orchestras,
  touring J-rock, big-room DJs, anything with an upcoming North American date.
  Include only when a "must surface" hook applies.

What does NOT count, and must be labelled if reported at all:
- **Tourist samplers** (Gion Corner at Yasaka Hall, hotel "cultural shows", Klook and
  Viator "experiences"). Report Gion Corner once, honestly, as a sampler, and rank it
  below any real performance of the same form. Never present a sampler as the real thing.
- **Karaoke, snack bars, hostess clubs, pachinko parlours.** Not performances.
- **Western-style club nights** with no Japan-specific angle.
- **Talk events, lectures, rakugo, manzai.** Not music.

Ranking within a tier: first, is this the real thing or a watered-down version (a
regular temple rite beats a "traditional music night" at a hotel); second, does it fit
the window and can a foreigner actually get in. **Fame, press, "best of Tokyo" lists and
Western reviews are not ranking inputs.**

### The "must surface" rule

Separately from the ranking, call out ANY event in the window that has a hook a
reasonable person would want to be told about even if the tier rules push it down:

- a centenary, anniversary, farewell, reunion, final performance, or debut
- a venue that is closing
- an artist who rarely or never plays live any more
- a once-a-year or once-in-N-years rite (a 式年 event, a shrine's grand festival year)
- anything sold out fast, or already sold out (say so; it is still information)

State the hook, then state where it landed in the ranking and why. Do not let the tier
rule silently bury one of these. The jazz pass buried a Miles Davis centenary at #7
because a "domestic first" rule was applied as a filter; a tier rule can do exactly the
same thing to, say, a farewell concert by a touring-scale act.

## EVIDENCE STANDARD

Mark every fact **VERIFIED** (you personally loaded the page or endpoint and read it)
or **SECONDARY** (search snippet, blog, aggregator, inference). Never present a
SECONDARY fact in the confident voice.

Specifically:
- **Dates for festivals and rites must come from the shrine, temple or organiser's own
  page for 2026**, not from last year's blog post. Many move with the lunar calendar or
  with the weekday. If the 2026 page is not up yet, say so and give last year's date
  labelled as such.
- **Availability must be a number or a status string you actually read**, not an
  inference from a presale announcement.
- **Prices must include everything**: entry + drink + seat + service %. Quote per-person
  and for-two figures. "Free" needs a source too (奉納 events are free; ticketed
  moon-viewings are not).
- If a venue publishes no set times, say so; do not substitute a house norm.
- Give a clickable URL for every source, including ones you checked and rejected.

## TECHNIQUE NOTES (carried over from the jazz pass, plus the traditional-arts layer)

- **A dead domain does not mean a dead venue.** Akasaka B-flat's old `bflat.jp` lapsed
  into a squatted parking page that fingerprints automation and serves click-fraud; the
  venue lives at `bflat.yamano-music.co.jp`. Mister Kelly's old domain is a squatted FX
  site. Always check whether a venue MOVED before reporting it unreachable, and confirm
  closures rather than assuming either way.
- **Curling a hostile domain to read it as text is safe** and is how you confirm it is
  parked. Never load one in a browser.
- **When a schedule page renders empty over HTTP, read its JS bundle for the data
  source** (B-flat's calendar was a microCMS feed with its public key in
  `/js/schedule.js`). Do this before resorting to browser automation.
- **Trust the booking system over the marketing page.** A "plans" page may describe
  products that are not purchasable or are agency-only.
- **Traditional-arts ticketing has its own traps:** the National Theatre ticket centre
  has an English site and takes foreign cards; noh theatres often sell by phone, e+, or
  往復はがき (return-postcard application), which a foreigner cannot do — flag postcard-only
  sales as "not bookable" rather than "no availability"; shrine rites are free but the
  seated area is sometimes 崇敬会 members only; Daikaku-ji-style moon-viewings sell
  advance tickets weeks out; kabuki 一幕見席 has a same-day queue component even when
  part is online.
- **Search in Japanese, not just English.** Aggregators to lean on for this layer:
  e+ (eplus.jp), Pia, Lawson, 能楽協会 / the-noh.com for noh schedules, 歌舞伎美人
  (kabuki-bito.jp) for kabuki, 国立劇場 / ntj.jac.go.jp for hōgaku and bunraku, and the
  shrine's own 年間行事 page for rites.
- **DuckDuckGo's HTML endpoint rate-limits after 1–2 queries.** Yahoo Japan through a
  browser holds up better for Japanese-language listings.
- **Run sequentially.** If a tool budget runs out, say so loudly in the output rather
  than quietly returning less.

## KNOWN FAILURE MODES TO DESIGN AGAINST

From the jazz pass:
1. **Reported a ticket that does not exist.** Read the marketing page, not the booking
   system. → Confirm what is actually purchasable.
2. **Inflated urgency from an inference.** Called a show near-sold-out because a presale
   had been announced; live data showed half the room free. → Read the numbers.
3. **Let a ranking rule bury a notable act.** → Apply the "must surface" rule.
4. **Parallel subagents exhausted the budget and coverage silently thinned.** → Run
   sequentially; report budget exhaustion.

New risks specific to this brief:
5. **Presenting a tourist sampler as the real form.** Gion Corner, hotel dinner shows and
   Klook "experiences" are not what this brief is after. Label them.
6. **Using last year's festival date.** Rites move with the moon and the weekday. → 2026
   source or nothing.
7. **Drifting back toward prestige.** If the shortlist starts filling with names that
   have Pitchfork reviews or a Brooklyn date next spring, the ranking axis has slipped.
   Re-read "WHAT CANNOT SEE STATESIDE MEANS" and re-rank.
8. **Reporting "no availability" when the truth is "not bookable by a foreigner."**
   These are different findings; the second one still tells the listeners to try in
   person or by phone from the hotel.
