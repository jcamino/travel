In Comillas

The three big monuments (El Capricho, Palacio de Sobrellano, Seminario Mayor) sit within a few hundred meters of each other, 2–3 km from Trasvía. Important: Sobrellano and the Seminario do not sell same-day tickets online — book the day before at centros.culturadecantabria.com, or take your chances at the box office.

El Capricho de Gaudí

Gaudí's 1883–85 orientalist summer villa — minaret tower, sunflower tiles, Persian and Mudéjar flourishes. One of only three Gaudí buildings outside Catalonia, and 2026 is officially "Gaudí Year".

Palacio de Sobrellano

Joan Martorell's neo-Gothic palace (1888) for the first Marqués de Comillas — marble staircase, polychrome stained glass, and Gaudí-designed furniture. Right next to El Capricho.

Seminario Mayor — former Universidad Pontificia

The monumental hilltop seminary (1883, Martorell) whose facade and doorway are by Lluís Domènech i Montaner — one of the great Catalan-Modernisme ensembles outside Catalonia, with sweeping views over Comillas and the coast. The closest monument to Trasvía.

Beaches, surf & the low-tide walk

Trasvía sits on the coast road between Comillas and Oyambre — both beaches are minutes away. This week has spring tides (coefficients 82–101), the best possible conditions for the low-tide coastal walk.

Playa de Comillas

The town beach: ~900 m of fine golden sand with a promenade to the harbor, gentle beginner-friendly waves.

Distance~2 km · ~5 min by car from Trasvía (or walk down through town)
LifeguardsCruz Roja daily 11:30–19:30 (15 Jun–15 Sep) Confirmed
FacilitiesBlue Flag · toilets, showers, sunshade/lounger rental · fully adapted access (amphibious chair available) · no dogs
PriceFree (paid parking lot adjacent)
  • El Capricho de Gaudí is only ~700 m away — easy to combine.
  • The parking lot fills very fast in August; arrive before ~09:30 or walk from town.
  • Sheltered, gentle surf — experienced surfers head to Oyambre instead.

Playa de Oyambre (& Playa de Gerra)

Nearly 2 km of wild sand backed by live dunes and the La Rabia estuary marshes, inside the Parque Natural de Oyambre — a designated World Surfing Reserve, with the Picos de Europa on the horizon.

Distance~3–4 km · ~5–7 min by car from Trasvía Estimate
LifeguardsJuly–August only
FacilitiesSeasonal beach bar + portable toilets at the main access · adapted access · otherwise unspoiled
PriceFree · parking lots off the CA-236 (last stretch partly unpaved); full by mid-morning in August
  • Birdwatching in the La Rabia estuary: herons, spoonbills, cormorants.
  • Playa de Gerra is the quieter western continuation of the same sand (access at La Gerruca, km 2.5 of the CA-236).
  • Never walk on the dunes — the system is protected and actively eroding.

Rasa mareal — the low-tide coastal walk

At low tide the sea peels back off a flat rocky platform of folded, layered strata between Comillas and Oyambre: tide pools full of crabs, urchins, anemones and the odd octopus. This week's spring tides expose it exceptionally well.

RouteDrop onto the platform at Playa de Comillas and head west toward the Ría de La Rabia and Oyambre; ~4–5 km one way. Clifftop path (Senda Costera, ~5.5 km circular) runs parallel as an escape/return route.

Safety: go out on the falling tide and be off the rocks within ~1.5–2 h after low — with coefficients this high the flood comes back fast and can cut you off against the cliffs. Closed non-slip shoes; skip it in swell or rain. If the tide catches you, climb to the Senda Costera clifftop path.
  • At the Oyambre end look for the retreating dune front (~1.3 m/yr), occasionally exposed ~5,000-year-old tree stumps, and the "Pájaro Amarillo" monument to a 1929 transatlantic flight's emergency landing.
  • Heights are relative to mean sea level (that's why lows are negative). Sunrise ~07:20, sunset ~21:20 all four days.
