A month on the Costa Tropical

Almuñécar

Phoenician town, Moorish castle, gray-shingle coves and Europe's only subtropical farm belt — everything worth knowing for a month here, in any season.

The year at a glance: air highs (WMO normals, Motril station 21 km east), sea temperature, and rain. Winters of 17–19 °C afternoons, a bone-dry June–August, and a sea worth swimming June–October.

Sexi: 3,000 years in one small town

In town

Phoenicians founded "Sexi" here around 800 BC; Rome left an aqueduct, a fish-sauce factory and two funerary towers; the Moors left the castle and the street plan. Almost all of it is walkable in an afternoon — and half of it is free. Note: the castle, museum and parks close Mondays; the tourist office never does.

Monument · old town hilltop · one ticket, two sites

Castillo de San Miguel + Cueva de Siete Palacios

The Nasrid-then-Christian fortress over the rooftops, with the city museum inside a vaulted 1st-century Roman substructure below it — home of the canopic vase of Pharaoh Apophis I, the oldest written document found on the Iberian Peninsula.

Casco antiguo

A whitewashed Moorish street plan climbing from Plaza de la Constitución to the castle gate — flower-hung lanes, tapas squares, and the fortified-looking Iglesia de la Encarnación (1595, the first Counter-Reformation church in Granada province, tower built with pirate raids in mind).

El Majuelo, the salting factory & the Peñón

At the castle's foot, a free subtropical botanical park wraps around the garum tanks of the Phoenician-Roman fish-salting factory that made Sexi rich — and hosts Jazz en la Costa each July. Two minutes away, the Peñón del Santo rock (daily 09:00–24:00, free) is the town's sunset balcony.

Free · open-air · half-day walk

Roman Almuñécar on foot

A 1st-century aqueduct still stands in five sections between town and the Río Verde valley — some astonishingly intact — plus Roman baths and two funerary towers, all free and fence-line visitable.

La Carrera section100 m, 17 arches at the town entrance, beside the excavated Roman termas — free, listed 09:00–24:00
Torrecuevas section130 m and 17 arches built into a plaza in Torrecuevas barrio (where October's chirimoya fiesta happens)
Ruta de los AcueductosThe official route links all five sections; allow half a day round-trip — pair with the two Roman columbaria (Torre del Monje + La Albina, ~1 km apart, always open)

Gray shingle, clear water

Beaches & water

Honesty first: this coast is dark sand and shingle, not golden dunes — what it trades for that is clear, deep, snorkel-grade water and cliff scenery. Five beaches flew the Blue Flag in 2026 (San Cristóbal, Puerta del Mar, Velilla, La Herradura, Marina del Este). Swimming season is roughly June–October: the sea peaks at 24–27 °C in August and bottoms out around 13–15 °C in February.

Town beaches · walk from anywhere

The Almuñécar beaches

Two bays split by the Peñón del Santo rock, then a string of coves west toward La Herradura.

San CristóbalThe classic: ~1 km of fine dark sand under the Peñón, full services, adapted access (new boardwalk in 2026), sunset side. Parking is the weak point.
Puerta del Mar1.1 km through the town center, gravel + dark sand, underground parking, packed in summer; the San Juan bonfires happen here.
Velilla~1.5 km, the family workhorse east of town: chiringuitos, children's areas, underground parking, the Aquatropic water park.
CotobroSmall, calm west-bay pebble beach that fills fast (<50 parking spots); showers but no toilets; El Balcón de Cotobro restaurant above.
Playa del MuertoThe naturist cove between Cotobro and La Herradura — cliff-backed, on foot only from Cotobro, essentially no services Sources conflict on a seasonal chiringuito

The west bay · ~7 km · 10 min

La Herradura & Marina del Este

A 2.2 km horseshoe bay sheltered between Punta de la Mona and Cerro Gordo — the region's watersports hub, facing the sunset. Around the headland, the Marina del Este cove is the snorkeling spot.

Protected coast · ~10 km · 15 min + shuttle

Cantarriján & the Maro-Cerro Gordo cliffs

A 1989-protected stretch of 75 m cliffs, hidden coves and a one-mile marine reserve on the Granada–Málaga border. Cantarriján is its serviced cove: gravel, crystalline water, naturist by tradition at the far end, and a legendary chiringuito.

15 minutes to 90 — a different Andalucía each day

Day trips

Almuñécar's real luxury is position: the Alhambra, Andalucía's prettiest village, a world-class cave, high sierra and two coastal castles all sit within ninety minutes. Driving times are free-flow OSRM figures — add 10–20% for traffic and parking.

City + palace · 80.3 km · ~1 h 10

Granada & the Alhambra

The one unmissable trip — and the one that punishes improvisation. Nasrid Palaces entry is a named, timed, ID-checked 30-minute slot, and peak dates sell out weeks to months ahead.

Cave + villages · 22–26 km · 25 min

Cueva de Nerja, Nerja & Frigiliana

A cathedral-scale cave with the world's largest natural column, the Balcón de Europa promenade, and — 7 km uphill — the whitewashed Mudéjar lanes of Frigiliana, serially voted Andalucía's prettiest village. The natural full-day loop west.

Fiestas, markets & harvests

The year here

Whatever month you pick, something is on. The two biggest draws: Jazz en la Costa in late July (one of Europe's great open-air jazz settings, under the castle) and the mid-August feria ending in a sea-borne procession and fireworks on the 15th.

Living here, not just visiting

A month here — the practical layer

Almuñécar works as a base: walkable town, a real daily market, buses that actually go places, and a health center in town. The famous "subtropical microclimate, 320 days of sun" line is a tourist-board claim, not an AEMET statistic — but the numbers don't argue with it.
